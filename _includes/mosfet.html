what do these symbols mean?
VGS, VDS
voltage from gate to source, and from drain to source. the source is the terminal the current leaves from (ground for an nmos here).
Vth
threshold voltage: the VGS at which a conducting channel of electrons starts to form under the gate.
Vov
overdrive voltage, VGS − Vth: how far the gate is above the threshold.
W, L
channel width and length. the current flows along L through a strip W wide, so a wide, short transistor passes more current.
k′ = µnCox
process constant: electron mobility µn (how easily electrons move) times oxide capacitance per area Cox (how strongly the gate controls the channel).
λ
channel-length modulation: a small correction because a bigger VDS shortens the effective channel a little.
gm, ro
small-signal quantities: gm = how much drain current changes per volt on the gate, ro = how much VDS is needed to change the current by one amp.
this is the simple long-channel “square-law” model, with no subthreshold leakage. it is great for intuition and first hand-calculations; a real 180 nm device has velocity saturation and other effects, so check against simulation.
