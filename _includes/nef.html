what do these symbols mean?
NEF (noise efficiency factor)
how many times more input noise (in rms volts) your amplifier has than an ideal single bipolar transistor stage that draws the same current and has the same bandwidth. NEF = 1 is that ideal reference; larger is worse.
PEF (power efficiency factor)
NEF² · VDD: the same idea but charging you for supply voltage as well, since power = current × VDD.
Vni,rms
input-referred noise: the noise at the output divided by the gain, integrated over the signal bandwidth, as an rms voltage. it is what you could not tell apart from a real input signal.
Itot, BW
total supply current of the amplifier (everything, not only the input pair) and its bandwidth in Hz.
UT
thermal voltage kT/q, about 26 mV at room temperature.
γ
thermal-noise factor of a MOS transistor, about 0.65 to 0.7 in both weak and strong inversion.
ε, x
ε: extra noise from the load and other devices, as a fraction of the input pair’s noise. x: extra supply current spent outside the input pair (bias, mirrors, common-mode feedback), as a fraction of the tail current.
fc
flicker (1/f) noise corner: the frequency below which 1/f noise exceeds the flat thermal noise. in biopotential bands (below 1 kHz) it often matters a lot.
for a basic MOS differential pair the thermal-only limit is a NEF of about 2 (it needs weak inversion). reaching 1 or below takes tricks such as current reuse or switched-capacitor stages, and reported values around 1 are the state of the art. the predictor assumes first-order band edges (noise bandwidth π/2 × (fH − fL)) and integrates flicker noise as S·fc·ln(fH/fL); it is a quick estimate, not a replacement for a noise simulation.
