cheat sheet: telling the region from node voltages
  • cutoff: VGS ≤ Vth (no channel at all).
  • triode: VGS > Vth and VDS < VGS − Vth.
  • saturation: VGS > Vth and VDS ≥ VGS − Vth.
  • same test with node voltages (nmos, source at ground): saturation needs VD ≥ VG − Vth. in words: the drain must stay high enough. equivalently, triode happens when the gate-to-drain voltage VGD is bigger than Vth, because then there is still a channel at the drain end.
  • pmos mirrors everything: triode if VSD < VSG − |Vthp|, i.e. the drain is more than |Vthp| above the gate: VD − VG > |Vthp|.
  • where it bites in real circuits: an output swinging too low (nmos) or too high (pmos), a load resistor or current that is too big, too many transistors stacked for the supply, a Vth that is higher than you assumed, or a gate driven harder than you planned.
