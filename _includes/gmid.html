what do these symbols mean?
gm
transconductance: how many amps of drain current you gain per volt on the gate. it is the “gain knob” of a transistor.
gm/ID
transconductance efficiency: gm divided by the current you spend. its unit is 1/V. high means a lot of gain and low noise for little current.
inversion coefficient IC
how strongly the channel is turned on, as the ratio of your current to a reference “specific current” IS of the same-shaped transistor. IC < 0.1 is weak inversion (subthreshold), 0.1 to 10 is moderate, above 10 is strong inversion (the square-law region you meet in textbooks).
UT, n
thermal voltage kT/q (about 26 mV at room temperature) and the subthreshold slope factor (about 1.2 to 1.5): how much gate voltage one e-fold of current costs in weak inversion is n·UT.
VA, ro
Early voltage and output resistance. a longer L gives a bigger VA, so a flatter I-V curve and more intrinsic gain.
fT
transit frequency: where the transistor’s current gain falls to 1, roughly gm divided by 2π times the gate capacitance. a rough measure of speed.
this uses the smooth EKV-style model with textbook-typical 180 nm numbers. it is meant for intuition and first sizing; real devices differ (velocity saturation, parasitics, short-channel effects), so replace it with a lookup table from your own simulations when you design for real.
